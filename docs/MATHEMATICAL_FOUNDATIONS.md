# TopoChain: Mathematical Foundations & Topological Invariant Drift Detection

This document outlines the rigorous mathematical and topological formulation underlying **TopoChain**, detailing how algebraic topology, simplicial complexes, persistent homology, and Topological Neural Networks (TNNs) quantify software evolution and detect supply chain compromises.

---

## 1. Graph Formulation of Software Architecture

Let a codebase snapshot at commit $t$ be represented by a weighted, directed multigraph $G_t = (V_t, E_t, w_t)$, where:
- Vertices $V_t$ represent software entities:
  - Internal functions, methods, and classes: $V_{\text{code}}$
  - Control flow blocks and sensitive operational sinks: $V_{\text{sink}}$
  - Direct and transitive external dependencies: $V_{\text{dep}}$
  $$V_t = V_{\text{code}} \cup V_{\text{sink}} \cup V_{\text{dep}}$$
- Directed edges $E_t \subseteq V_t \times V_t$ represent structural relationships:
  - Function invocations and call graphs ($u \xrightarrow{\text{call}} v$)
  - Module containment and inheritance ($u \xrightarrow{\text{contains}} v$)
  - External package usage and runtime hooks ($u \xrightarrow{\text{dep}} v$)
- Edge metric weights $w_t: E_t \to \mathbb{R}^+$ define an architectural distance metric:
  $$w(u, v) = \psi(u, v) \cdot \left(1 + \alpha \ln(1 + \mathcal{C}(u, v))\right)$$
  where:
  - $\psi(u, v)$ encodes structural locality:
    $$\psi(u, v) = \begin{cases} 
      0.15 & \text{if } u \text{ contains } v \\
      0.20 & \text{if } u, v \text{ are co-located in the same module} \\
      0.50 & \text{if } u, v \text{ span distinct internal modules} \\
      0.75 & \text{if } v \in V_{\text{dep}} \text{ (external dependency invocation)}
    \end{cases}$$
  - $\mathcal{C}(u, v) = \max(\text{CyclomaticComplexity}(u), \text{CyclomaticComplexity}(v))$
  - If $u$ or $v$ touches security-sensitive sinks (`eval`, `subprocess`, `urllib`, `socket`), the distance is modulated to accentuate structural geodesic centrality.

The graph is symmetrized into an undirected metric space $(X_t, d_t)$, where $d_t(u, v)$ is the shortest path geodesic distance between vertices $u, v \in V_t$, bounded by diameter threshold $D_{\max} = 1.0$.

## 2. Simplicial Complexes & Clique Filtration

A **simplicial complex** $K$ is a collection of simplices (generalizations of triangles to arbitrary dimensions) such that:
1. Every face of a simplex in $K$ is also in $K$.
2. The non-empty intersection of any two simplices $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 \in K$ is a face of both.

### Clique Complex (Flag Complex)
Given the code graph $G_t$, the **clique complex** $\text{Cl}(G_t)$ is the simplicial complex whose $k$-simplices correspond to all complete subgraphs ($k+1$ mutually adjacent vertices) of $G_t$:
$$\sigma = [v_0, v_1, \dots, v_k] \in \text{Cl}(G_t) \iff \forall i \neq j, \; (v_i, v_j) \in E_t$$

### Vietoris-Rips Filtration
To observe the multiscale shape of the code, we construct a **Vietoris-Rips filtration** parameterized by scale parameter $\epsilon \in [0, D_{\max}]$:
$$\text{VR}_\epsilon(X_t) = \{ \sigma \subseteq V_t \mid \forall u, v \in \sigma, \; d_t(u, v) \le \epsilon \}$$

As $\epsilon$ monotonically increases, we obtain a nested sequence of simplicial complexes:
$$\emptyset = K^0 \subseteq K^{\epsilon_1} \subseteq K^{\epsilon_2} \subseteq \dots \subseteq K^{D_{\max}} = K$$

## 3. Persistent Homology & Invariant Extraction

For each dimension $k \ge 0$, the $k$-th chain group $C_k(K)$ is the vector space generated by $k$-simplices over field $\mathbb{Z}_2$.
The boundary operator $\partial_k: C_k(K) \to C_{k-1}(K)$ maps each $k$-simplex to its oriented $(k-1)$-faces:
$$\partial_k [v_0, \dots, v_k] = \sum_{i=0}^k (-1)^i [v_0, \dots, \hat{v}_i, \dots, v_k]$$

Crucially, $\partial_k \circ \partial_{k+1} = 0$, guaranteeing that the image of $\partial_{k+1}$ (boundaries $B_k$) is a subspace of the kernel of $\partial_k$ (cycles $Z_k$):
$$B_k = \text{im}(\partial_{k+1}) \subseteq Z_k = \ker(\partial_k)$$

The $k$-th **homology group** is defined as the quotient space:
$$H_k(K) = Z_k / B_k$$
Its dimension is the $k$-th **Betti number** $\beta_k = \dim(H_k(K))$:
- $\beta_0$: Number of connected components (isolated submodules, dependencies).
- $\beta_1$: Number of 1-dimensional topological loops/cycles (circular call chains, recursive feedback loops, stealth hooks).
- $\beta_2$: Number of 2-dimensional voids/cavities (higher-order circular interactions between triangles).

### Persistence Diagrams
Across the filtration parameter $\epsilon$, a topological feature $\alpha \in H_k$ is born at $\epsilon = b$ and dies (merges into a boundary) at $\epsilon = d$.
This yields the **persistence diagram** $D_k$:
$$D_k = \{ (b_i, d_i) \mid b_i < d_i \}$$
with persistence (lifetime) defined as $\text{pers}(\alpha_i) = d_i - b_i$.

### Stability Theorem
Persistent homology is mathematically stable under perturbation. By the Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Harer Stability Theorem:
$$d_B(D(f), D(g)) \le \|f - g\|_\infty$$
where $d_B$ is the Bottleneck distance:
$$d_B(D_1, D_2) = \inf_{\gamma} \sup_{p \in D_1} \|p - \gamma(p)\|_\infty$$
This fundamental theorem guarantees that benign refactoring and superficial code renaming cause only bounded, infinitesimal fluctuations in persistence diagrams. In contrast, **malicious logic injections alter graph connectivity and introduce macroscopic topological defects**.

## 4. Multi-Channel Persistence Images

To feed persistence diagrams into neural networks with fixed-dimensional spatial grids, we transform diagrams $D_0, D_1, D_2$ into multi-channel 2D **Persistence Images** $\rho_k: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^+$:

1. Transform coordinates from birth-death $(b, d)$ to birth-persistence $(b, p = d - b)$.
2. Apply a variance-preserving weight function $w(b, p)$ that discounts zero-lifetime noise:
   $$w(b, p) = \min\left(1.0, \frac{p}{p_{\max}}\right)$$
3. Compute continuous density via isotropic Gaussian kernels:
   $$\rho_k(x, y) = \sum_{(b_i, p_i) \in D_k} w(b_i, p_i) \frac{1}{2\pi \sigma^2} \exp\left(-\frac{(x - b_i)^2 + (y - p_i)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$
4. Discretize into a resolution grid $R \times R$ ($32 \times 32$) and stack across dimensions:
   $$\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 32 \times 32} \quad \text{where channel 0: } H_0, \text{ channel 1: } H_1, \text{ channel 2: } H_2$$

## 5. Topological Neural Network (TNN) & Manifold Embedding

Let $\phi_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 32 \times 32} \to \mathbb{S}^{63} \subset \mathbb{R}^{64}$ be a deep Topological Neural Network parameterized by weights $\theta$.

### Architecture
- **Multi-Scale Convolutional Backbone:** Conv2D + BatchNorm + SiLU with residual connections extracting local and global topological motifs.
- **Dual Global Aggregation:** Concatenation of Global Average Pooling (overall topological density) and Global Max Pooling (peak persistence spikes).
- **Hypersphere Projection:**
  $$\mathbf{z} = \frac{\mathbf{W}_2 \cdot \text{LayerNorm}(\text{SiLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \cdot \mathbf{h}))}{\|\mathbf{W}_2 \cdot \text{LayerNorm}(\text{SiLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \cdot \mathbf{h}))\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{63}$$

### Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning
The network is trained to map benign evolutionary trajectories into a smooth manifold while repelling structural defects:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}} = \max\left(0, \|\mathbf{z}_{\text{anchor}} - \mathbf{z}_{\text{pos}}\|_2^2 - \|\mathbf{z}_{\text{anchor}} - \mathbf{z}_{\text{neg}}\|_2^2 + m\right)$$
where:
- $\mathbf{z}_{\text{anchor}}, \mathbf{z}_{\text{pos}}$ are consecutive benign commits.
- $\mathbf{z}_{\text{neg}}$ is a commit subjected to synthetic supply chain perturbation (backdoor hooking, typosquatted dependency).
- $m$ is the contrastive margin ($m = 0.5$).

## 6. Topological Drift & Statistical Anomaly Scoring

At commit $t$, given current embedding $\mathbf{z}_t$ and previous embedding $\mathbf{z}_{t-1}$:
1. **Topological Drift Metric:**
   $$\Delta_t = \|\mathbf{z}_t - \mathbf{z}_{t-1}\|_2$$
2. **Manifold Baseline Statistics:**
   For a historical sequence of benign commits, the running mean $\mu_t$ and variance $\sigma_t^2$ of $\Delta$ are maintained incrementally via Welford's algorithm:
   $$\mu_t = \mu_{t-1} + \frac{\Delta_t - \mu_{t-1}}{N}, \quad M_{2, t} = M_{2, t-1} + (\Delta_t - \mu_{t-1})(\Delta_t - \mu_t), \quad \sigma_t^2 = \frac{M_{2, t}}{N - 1}$$
3. **Normalized Anomaly Z-Score:**
   $$S_t = \frac{\Delta_t - \mu_t}{\sqrt{\sigma_t^2 + \epsilon}}$$
4. **Decision Boundary:**
   $$\text{Verdict}(t) = \begin{cases}
     \text{ANOMALY\_DETECTED (Alert / Block CI)} & \text{if } S_t > \tau \text{ or } |\text{Defects}| > 0 \\
     \text{PASSED (Update Baseline)} & \text{otherwise}
   \end{cases}$$
   where $\tau = 3.0$ defines the 99.7% confidence boundary under normal evolutionary variation.
